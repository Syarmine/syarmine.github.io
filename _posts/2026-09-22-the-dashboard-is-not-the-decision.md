Most organisations now have more dashboards than decisions. The reporting is faster, cleaner and more frequent than it has ever been. Yet senior teams often feel less sure of what to do. Part of the reason is what dashboards leave out.

## A single number hides a range

A dashboard tile shows a number: waiting time, occupancy, cost per case. What it rarely shows is how confident we should be in that number, or how much it would move under a different but equally reasonable assumption. The tile looks precise. The underlying estimate often is not.

## Three habits that help

1. **Show the range, not just the point.** A simple band around a line chart changes how people read it.
2. **Separate signal from noise.** Not every month-on-month change deserves a meeting. Agree in advance what size of change would trigger action.
3. **Name the decision.** Every recurring report should state which decision it informs. If nobody can name one, the report can probably go.

## Uncertainty is information

Leaders sometimes ask analysts to remove caveats because they make the message less clear. The opposite is true. Knowing that an estimate could plausibly be 20 per cent higher or lower is exactly what a decision-maker needs to judge risk. Stripping it out does not make the decision easier. It makes it worse, and hides that it is worse.
