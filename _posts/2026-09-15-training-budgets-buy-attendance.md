Organisations measure training by what is easy to count: enrolments, completion rates, hours delivered, satisfaction scores. None of these tell you whether people can now do something they could not do before, or whether they are doing it at work.

## Completion is not competence

A course can have near-perfect completion and change nothing. People attend, pass a quiz and return to the same workflow with the same tools and the same incentives. The skill decays within weeks because nothing in the job asks for it.

## Measure use, not attendance

Better programmes are judged on what changes in the work:

- **Is the skill being used?** Look at the work product, not the certificate. Are analyses, reports or decisions different?
- **Did the bottleneck move?** If training was meant to reduce dependence on a central team, has the request queue to that team shrunk?
- **Is it sustained?** Check again at three and six months. Most of the drop-off happens after the course ends.

## Design the job, not just the course

Capability sticks when the job requires it: when the new tool is the default, when managers ask for the new output, and when someone is available to help in the first weeks. Without those, the training budget buys a good day out.
